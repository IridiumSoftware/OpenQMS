# Hazard Communication Written Program

**Template for:** OSHA HazCom (29 CFR 1910.1200(e)(1)) — workplace chemical hazard communication program.

**Compliance dates:** Substance manufacturer/importer 2026-01-19; mixture manufacturer/importer 2027-07-19; downstream employer updates within reasonable time after receiving updated SDS.

## 3. Labels and other forms of warning — §1910.1200(f)

### 3.1 Supplier labels (containers as received)

Labels from manufacturer / importer / distributor must contain per HCS 2024:
- Product identifier
- Signal word (Danger or Warning)
- Hazard statement(s)
- Pictogram(s) — 8 GHS pictograms with red diamond border (US convention same as EU CLP)
- Precautionary statement(s)
- Supplier name + address + phone

**Inspection at receipt:** [SOP reference — receiver checks supplier label compliance before placing into use; non-compliant containers held + supplier notified].

### 3.2 Workplace labels (in-house transfers)

Alternative formats permitted per §1910.1200(f)(6) provided equivalent information conveyed. Permitted approaches:
- Full supplier-style label
- Workplace label with product identifier + words/pictures/symbols/combination + general information sufficient for employee to identify chemical + hazards
- NFPA 704 / HMIS in conjunction with employee training that bridges system-to-HCS

**This site uses:** [option chosen + reference to workplace-label template / printing setup].

### 3.3 Portable container exemption

Portable container filled from a labeled stationary container intended only for immediate use of the employee who performs the transfer — exempt per §1910.1200(f)(8). Employees trained that "immediate use" means same work shift + sole control of contents.

### 3.4 Pipes + piping systems

Per §1910.1200(e)(1)(ii): methods used to inform employees of hazards of chemicals contained in unlabeled pipes in their work areas. [Site approach: pipe-marking standard (ASME A13.1 referenced); training during initial assignment + before reassignment to unfamiliar areas.]

## 4. Safety Data Sheets — §1910.1200(g)

### 4.1 SDS collection

SDS maintained for each hazardous chemical in the inventory. Format:
- Paper binder at [location(s)]
- Electronic + backup at [URL / share path]
- Mobile access: [yes/no — if yes, app/portal + tested no-connectivity fallback]

**Accessibility.** SDS readily accessible to employees during EACH WORK SHIFT when they are in their work area. Test annually: random employee + random chemical + time-to-access ≤ 5 minutes target.

### 4.2 SDS receipt + maintenance

| Trigger | Action | SLA |
|---|---|---|
| New chemical incoming | Verify SDS in shipment OR request before unloading | Before first use |
| SDS missing | Written request to supplier; do not use chemical | 24 hours to request |
| Supplier reissues SDS | Replace in collection; update inventory if class. changes | Within 7 days of receipt |
| Annual audit | All chemicals have current SDS; obsolete chemicals removed | Annual |

### 4.3 SDS format

16-section per UN GHS Annex 4 / HCS Appendix D / EU REACH Annex II / EU CLP. See `SAFETY-DATA-SHEET-TEMPLATE.md` for authoring template if site produces or repackages chemicals.

## 5. Employee information + training — §1910.1200(h)

### 5.1 When training is delivered

- At initial assignment to a work area where hazardous chemicals are present
- Whenever a NEW chemical hazard is introduced into the work area (not just a new chemical — new HAZARD; e.g., switching from solvent-based to water-based formulation does not trigger retraining unless the new product introduces a hazard category employees weren't already trained on)
- Refresher cadence: [annual recommended; document rationale if longer]

### 5.2 Information delivered

- Requirements of HCS
- Operations in employee's work area where hazardous chemicals present
- Location + availability of: written program, hazardous chemical list, SDS collection

### 5.3 Training content

- Methods + observations used to detect presence or release of hazardous chemicals in the work area
- Physical hazards (17 classes per Appendix B)
- Health hazards (10 classes per Appendix A)
- Simple asphyxiation, combustible dust, pyrophoric gas, hazards-not-otherwise-classified
- Protective measures: work practices, emergency procedures, PPE
- Details of written program: how to read labels + SDSs

## 6. Non-routine tasks — §1910.1200(e)(1)(ii)

Methods used to inform employees of hazards of non-routine tasks. Examples: confined-space entry; equipment maintenance involving exposure to residual chemicals; emergency response to spills/releases.

**Approach.** Job hazard analysis (JHA) preceding non-routine task. JHA references chemicals present + applicable SDS sections + required PPE + emergency procedures. JHA reviewed in pre-task briefing; signed by all participants.

## 7. Multi-employer workplaces — §1910.1200(e)(2)

When other employer's employees may be exposed to hazardous chemicals at this site (e.g., contractors, vendors, delivery personnel):

- This site provides: (i) access to applicable SDSs, (ii) information on labelling system + workplace labels in use, (iii) information on precautionary measures.
- This site obtains: (i) information on hazardous chemicals brought onto site by other employers' employees, (ii) information on labelling system used + precautionary measures recommended.

**Operationalization.** Contractor onboarding includes HCS-information-exchange checklist. SDS for contractor chemicals collected at site entry; contractor receives site SDS access procedure + workplace label key.

## 8. Trade secrets — §1910.1200(i)

If withholding specific chemical identity from SDS as trade secret:
- Claim must be supportable per Appendix E criteria
- Other required information must be disclosed
- SDS must indicate that specific chemical identity is being withheld as trade secret

**Health professional + employee access during medical emergency.** Specific chemical identity disclosed immediately to treating physician / nurse on need-to-know without confidentiality agreement; written confirmation within reasonable time post-emergency.

**Non-emergency access.** Specific chemical identity disclosed to health professional / employee / employee representative on written request with statement of need + written confidentiality agreement per §1910.1200(i)(3)-(11).
