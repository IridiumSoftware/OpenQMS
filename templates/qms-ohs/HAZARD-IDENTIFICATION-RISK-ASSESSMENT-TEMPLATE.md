### Hierarchy of controls (apply in order — ISO 45001 §8.1.2.1)

1. **Elimination** — remove the hazard entirely (preferred; if achievable, all other tiers become unnecessary for this hazard).
2. **Substitution** — replace with less hazardous material, process, or equipment.
3. **Engineering controls** — guards, interlocks, ventilation, isolation, ergonomic redesign.
4. **Administrative controls + reorganization of work** — procedures, training, signage, work rotation, permit-to-work systems.
5. **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)** — gloves, eyewear, respirators, hearing protection. PPE is the *last* resort, not the default control.

Each additional control proposed must justify its tier ("why not a higher tier?").

## 5. Typical hazard categories

- **Physical:** mechanical (crush, cut, struck-by, slip/trip/fall, working at height, falling objects), electrical, pressure, temperature (hot/cold work, thermal radiation), noise, vibration, radiation (ionizing + non-ionizing), confined spaces.
- **Chemical:** corrosive, toxic, flammable, reactive, sensitizer, carcinogen. Cross-reference SDS register.
- **Biological:** infectious agents, allergens, biohazardous waste, animals/pests.
- **Ergonomic:** manual handling, repetitive motion, awkward postures, sustained force, workstation layout.
- **Psychosocial:** work demands + workload, lack of control over work, social environment, bullying + harassment, fatigue, shift work, traumatic event exposure.
- **Work environment:** lighting, indoor air quality, thermal comfort, housekeeping, layout, isolation/lone work.
- **Organizational:** competence gaps, communication failure, change management gaps, contractor management, emergency-response readiness.
