# HIPAA Reproductive Health Care Privacy Attestation

**Template for:** HIPAA Privacy Rule §164.509 Attestation. Required by HHS Final Rule strengthening HIPAA Privacy Rule protections for Reproductive Health Care (89 FR 32976, April 26, 2024; effective December 23, 2024; compliance for most provisions February 16, 2026 — verify HHS announcements for adjusted dates).
**Scope:** Required when a CE / BA receives a request from a person for PHI **potentially related to reproductive health care** for one of the following purposes:
- (a) Health oversight activities under §164.512(d)
- (b) Judicial + administrative proceedings under §164.512(e)
- (c) Law enforcement purposes under §164.512(f)
- (d) Disclosures to coroners + medical examiners under §164.512(g)(1)

**Prohibition:** §164.502(a)(5)(iii) — CE / BA may NOT use or disclose PHI to investigate or impose liability on any person for the mere act of seeking / obtaining / providing / facilitating reproductive health care that was lawful in the state in which it was provided OR is lawful under federal law.

## 2. Threshold determination — is the request for PHI POTENTIALLY related to reproductive health care?

Per §164.509(a), "reproductive health care" defined broadly per §164.103 — care, services, supplies related to the reproductive system + its functions + processes (contraception, pregnancy-related, fertility, gender-affirming, miscarriage management, abortion, sexually transmitted infection, sexual health, post-partum, etc.).

| Question | Response |
|---|---|
| Could the PHI requested potentially relate to reproductive health care (even partially)? | ☐ Yes — Attestation REQUIRED before any disclosure ☐ No — standard §164.512 processing |
| Is the request specifically for PHI of reproductive health care services or related records? | ☐ Yes ☐ No (but PHI could still incidentally relate) |

If **Yes** → Attestation per §164.509 REQUIRED. Disclosure WITHOUT signed Attestation is PROHIBITED.

### 3.2 Required attestation statement (§164.509(c)(1)(v))

> [Signatory] attests that the use or disclosure of the PHI is NOT for any of the following prohibited purposes:
>
> (i) To conduct a criminal, civil, or administrative investigation into any person for the mere act of seeking, obtaining, providing, or facilitating reproductive health care; OR
>
> (ii) To impose criminal, civil, or administrative liability on any person for the mere act of seeking, obtaining, providing, or facilitating reproductive health care; OR
>
> (iii) The identification of any person for either of the purposes described in (i) or (ii).
>
> [Signatory] further attests that the reproductive health care to which the requested information relates was lawful under the circumstances in which it was provided + that the requested PHI is needed for a permitted purpose under 45 CFR 164.512(d) / (e) / (f) / (g)(1) (as applicable).

### 3.3 Notice of penalties for false attestation (§164.509(c)(1)(vi))

> Notice: Knowingly providing false statements in this attestation may subject the signer to criminal penalties under 42 USC §1320d-6 (up to $50,000 + 1 year imprisonment for knowing misuse; up to $100,000 + 5 years for false pretenses; up to $250,000 + 10 years for intent to sell, transfer, or use for commercial advantage / personal gain / malicious harm). Civil penalties may also apply.

## 4. Validity check before disclosure

| Defect | Status |
|---|---|
| Attestation incomplete per §3 above | ☐ Complete ☐ INCOMPLETE — DO NOT disclose |
| Attestation appears to combine multiple unrelated requests (compound attestations prohibited per §164.509) | ☐ Single-request ☐ COMPOUND — DO NOT disclose |
| CE/BA has actual knowledge that attestation contains false information | ☐ No knowledge ☐ KNOWN FALSE — DO NOT disclose; document |
| CE/BA has substantial evidence indicating attestation contains false information | ☐ No evidence ☐ SUBSTANTIAL EVIDENCE — Consider further inquiry before disclosure |

If ANY defect → **DO NOT DISCLOSE.** Provide written response to requestor explaining basis for refusal.
